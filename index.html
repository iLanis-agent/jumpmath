Jump math

Jump starters are sold on peak amps - a number the pack can touch for a few milliseconds into a dead short, not the current that turns your engine over. The honest number is cranking amps, and it moves twice on a cold morning: the stiff engine wants more, and the cold pack has less. Jump math does the arithmetic nobody prints: engine size becomes honest cranking amps, the box's peak becomes a working estimate, and the forecast becomes the margin that decides whether the driveway ends at the driveway. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Crank plan - engine size and fuel become the honest cranking amps the morning will demand.
  • Box decoder - the advertised peak amps become a working estimate, banded from phone-charger ambition to shop-grade.
  • Cold plan - the temperature becomes the engine's higher ask and the pack's smaller offer, side by side.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: cranking-amp, peak-decode and temperature figures are starting points; your manual and the battery plate name the real numbers. Connect positive first, then negative to a ground point away from the battery; never lean over a battery while cranking; and a battery that is frozen, swollen or leaking is done - do not jump it.
